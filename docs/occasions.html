Change Ringing Corpus · Second analytical output

Why people ring

Every performance is woven with human intent. By classifying hundreds of thousands of footnotes, we can map the overarching reasons why bells are rung—and the physical endurance those occasions demand—all while strictly preserving individual privacy.

Seasonality

The cadence of ringing occasions throughout the calendar year. Notice the huge spikes for Christmas and New Year, compared to the stable year-round cadence of Birthday performances.

The Occasions Ledger

Total performances categorised by underlying motivation. The overwhelming majority of dedicated ringing is to celebrate personal achievements and milestones.

Endurance of Intent

The distribution of changes rung (length of performance) across different occasion types. By plotting every performance, we can see the physical endurance demanded by different motivations.

There are two distinct peaks in change ringing: the Quarter Peal (typically ~1,260 changes, taking 45 minutes) and the grueling Full Peal (typically ~5,040 changes, taking 3 hours of non-stop concentration).

When we split these performances by their occasion, stark patterns emerge. Memorials and Church Festivals are predominantly commemorated with shorter Quarter Peals. However, Royal events and Firsts/Milestones show a significantly higher proportion of 3-hour Full Peals, reflecting the immense physical effort ringers put into celebrating national events and personal achievements.
