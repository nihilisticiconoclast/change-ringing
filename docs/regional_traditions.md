# Regional traditions: the practice is Cornwall's, the name is Devon's

**Roadmap item 25.** Measured on the 2012–2024 corpus, 293,471 performances.
Reproduce with `python scripts/classify_regional_traditions.py --full --query`,
which reclassifies from the database, rewrites
`data/regional_traditions_classified.csv`, and runs the recorded query in
[`queries/findings/regional_traditions_normalised.sql`](../queries/findings/regional_traditions_normalised.sql).

64,993 performances name something the method resolver could not match to the
CCCBR library. Grouping those by raw string fragments one practice across dozens
of spellings — `Call Changes`, `Call-Changes`, `Callchanges`, `Devon Call
Changes` — so the earlier finding that "Devon Call Changes is 85.4% concentrated
in Devon" was measuring a **local naming convention**, and doing it on 96
records. This normalises the strings into practices and asks the question again.

## The classifier, and how far it can be trusted

A regex classifier over seven categories: `tolling`, `call_changes`,
`rounds_and_call_changes`, `rounds`, `general`, `multiple_methods`, and
`unclassified` for anything that is a named method it could not resolve.

**98.5% accurate against 200 rows labelled independently by hand**
(`data/regional_traditions_oracle.csv`, drawn with a seeded sample so it can be
redrawn). All three disagreements run the same way — the classifier declining to
categorise something a reader would have categorised:

| Method text | Hand label | Classifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `(2 method 1 variation)` | `multiple_methods` | `unclassified` |
| `Four Doubles` | `multiple_methods` | `unclassified` |
| `Grandsire Caters and Rounds on 12` | `multiple_methods` | `rounds` |

Not one is a wrong practice — no record was put in `call_changes` or `tolling`
that does not belong there. **Every count below is therefore a lower bound**,
which is the safe direction for a concentration claim.

> **The first version of this measurement was worthless and said so.** Its
> 400-row "hand-labelled" oracle had been generated by running the classifier and
> spot-checking the output, which produced F1 = 1.00 on every category. Scored
> properly, that oracle disagreed with the classifier on **1 row out of 400** —
> it was 99.75% the classifier's own output, so it measured nothing at all. The
> oracle here was labelled from the method strings alone, before the classifier
> was run over them, and disagrees on 3 of 200. For comparison, the genuinely
> independent oracle behind the footnote classifier disagrees on 98 of 400.
>
> The circular oracle also hid a real defect: matching only the spaced form of
> "call change" dropped 115 records written `Call-Changes` or `Callchanges`. A
> circular oracle cannot find a recall bug, because the labels have the same
> blind spot as the code.

## The denominator is the finding

The obvious query divides a county's count by the **national total** for the
practice. That does not measure tradition — it measures how much a county
reports overall, so a county that reports a lot of everything ranks high in
every category. The right question is: *of what this county reports, how much is
this practice?*

Both are below. They disagree about almost everything.

### Call changes — 6,455 performances

| County | Performances | County's total output | **% of county** | % of national |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Cornwall** | 848 | 3,332 | **25.45%** | 13.14% |
| Lincolnshire | 649 | 8,518 | 7.62% | 10.05% |
| Staffordshire | 242 | 4,451 | 5.44% | 3.75% |
| **Devon** | 473 | 8,808 | **5.37%** | 7.33% |
| Suffolk | 333 | 8,489 | 3.92% | 5.16% |

**A quarter of everything Cornwall reports is call changes.** That is 3.3× the
next county and by far the strongest regional signal anywhere in this corpus.
Devon — whose name is attached to the practice throughout ringing — is fourth,
at 5.37%, statistically indistinguishable from Staffordshire.

So the received wisdom has the right practice and the wrong county. "Devon Call
Changes" is a *style* named after Devon; the county that actually rings call
changes as a substantial share of its ringing is Cornwall.

### Tolling — 7,877 performances

| County | Performances | **% of county** | % of national |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Northamptonshire** | 322 | **6.72%** | 4.09% |
| Bedfordshire | 175 | 6.24% | 2.22% |
| Merseyside | 95 | 5.38% | 1.21% |
| Lancashire | 109 | 5.30% | 1.38% |
| Tyne and Wear | 58 | 5.24% | 0.74% |

The earlier reading — that Lincolnshire is the home of tolling, following the
`Quick Tolling` string being 98.8% Lincolnshire — **does not survive**.
Lincolnshire is not in the top five once each county is measured against its own
output. `Quick Tolling` is a Lincolnshire *phrase*; tolling is not a Lincolnshire
practice.

## What would change this answer

- **The corpus is reported performances, not ringing.** Call changes before a
  Sunday service are almost never reported to BellBoard. Cornwall's 25% is 25%
  of what Cornwall *reports*, and a county whose bands report their call-change
  ringing will outrank one that rings just as much and reports peals only. This
  is the same limit as the [two populations](two_populations.md) result.
- **`unclassified` is 13,772 records**, most of them named methods the resolver
  could not match. If any practice hides in there disproportionately in one
  county, these shares move.
- **County comes from the tower, via Dove.** Performances with no resolvable
  tower are absent, and a county boundary is not a ringing boundary — the Devon
  call-change tradition does not stop at the Cornwall border in either direction.
