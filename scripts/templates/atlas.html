I · Territory

Foundries had catchment areas, and you can see them

Each dot is a tower, placed by its own coordinates and coloured by the foundry tradition that cast most of its bells. All 12,428 towers are shown in one colour to begin with, because eight hues scattered across a map is unreadable — select a tradition and it lights up against the rest. Rudhall of Gloucester is the clearest case: a dense cluster through Gloucestershire, Herefordshire and the Welsh marches, and almost nothing beyond it. The firm cast 2,745 surviving bells between 1679 and 1835 without ever becoming national. Loughborough and Whitechapel, by contrast, are everywhere — the railways let them be.

II · Succession

Two foundries outlived everyone else

Surviving bells by quarter-century and tradition. This counts bells still hanging, not bells cast — losses, recastings and wartime scrap fall out of the record, so the deep past is thinner than it truly was.
